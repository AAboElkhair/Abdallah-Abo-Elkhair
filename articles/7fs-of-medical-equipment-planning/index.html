The 7Fs of Medical Equipment Planning: A Simple Framework for Assessing Equipment Needs

18 November 2026 · 2 min read

Medical equipment planningFrameworkHealthcare planning

When assessing the need for medical equipment, it's important to consider several factors. In this infographic, I've summarized the most important factors in an easy-to-remember way using 7 words, all starting with the letter 'F' (7Fs).

1. Functionality

It's crucial to determine the equipment's clinical need and clearly understand its functions. The guiding questions are: what are the needs, and what does the equipment do? Starting from the clinical need keeps the equipment list tied to the services the facility will provide.

2. Frequency

Consider how often the equipment will be used. How often an item is expected to be used is one of the inputs that helps decide how many units are needed.

3. Fitments

Determine the pre-installation requirements of the equipment to ensure effective and efficient operation and decide on its placement in the space. The guiding question is: what does the equipment need in order to work? This is where space, utilities and other installation requirements are identified before the equipment arrives.

4. Financial

Consider both the cost of the equipment and the expected revenue. The guiding questions are: how much will it cost, and what is the expected return?

5. Feasibility assessment

Assess equipment reuse options, availability for relocation in the future, and the equipment's return on investment period. In other words, what are the reuse and relocation options available?

6. Future readiness

Consider anticipated technological changes for the equipment that can help in future-proofing and ensure the equipment remains relevant. The guiding question is: what technology changes do we expect for the equipment?

7. Functional lifespan

Determine the useful life of the equipment. How long is the equipment's useful life?

Using the 7Fs together

The seven factors work best as a checklist applied to each piece of equipment, rather than one at a time. They are connected: the answers on fitments, financial and functional lifespan, for example, all feed into the same decision about whether and how to include an item in the plan.

I hope you find the 7Fs framework useful. I'd love to hear your thoughts on this approach. Have you used a similar framework? Do you have additional factors that need to be considered? Share your experience and insights with me on LinkedIn.
