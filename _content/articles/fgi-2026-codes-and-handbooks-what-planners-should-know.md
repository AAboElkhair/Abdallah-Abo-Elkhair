For decades, one document did two jobs. Regulators read it as the requirement. Designers read it as advice. The 2026 FGI editions take a clear step to separate those jobs.

## What changed in the structure

FGI (the Facility Guidelines Institute) released the 2026 FGI Codes for Planning and Design together with new FGI Handbooks for Planning and Design, as reported by Healthcare Design magazine on 1 September 2026. The same report says the codes now focus exclusively, for the first time, on minimum planning and design requirements.

An FGI article published in HFM magazine describes the same split: mandatory requirements are separated from supplemental guidance, with three independent codes (hospitals, outpatient settings, and residential care and support settings). The codes present the minimum requirements intended for adoption and enforcement, while the handbooks provide context, illustrations, examples and explanations of the rationale.

![Infographic: the 2026 FGI structure. Three codes carry minimum requirements for adoption and enforcement; handbooks carry context and examples](images/fgi-2026-structure.webp)

## What did not change

According to the HFM article, requirements were not rewritten as part of the reorganization, only relocated. The old general project requirements were reorganized into five chapters: Introduction, Planning, Design, Construction and Commissioning. The glossary was reviewed and language aligned across all three codes. The 2026 editions also incorporate the 2025 edition of ANSI/ASHRAE/ASHE Standard 170 on ventilation of health care facilities, according to the same article.

## Why it matters to planners

Equipment planning lives where requirements meet intent. Room data sheets, space allowances, clearances and utility requirements all trace back to what the code says, and to why.

- **Cite the code for what is required, the handbook for why.** A clear split makes design review comments easier to justify.
- **Check which edition your jurisdiction enforces.** Adoption is a regulatory step. A new publication does not automatically replace the edition a project is bound to, so confirm with the authority having jurisdiction and the contract documents.
- **Update templates and checklists.** Room data sheet notes and review checklists that cite section numbers should be checked against the new chapter organization.
- **Keep a record of the edition per project.** Writing the edition in the project information avoids confusion on long programs.

## Access

Healthcare Design reports that the 2026 editions are available on FGI OneSource, a new digital platform. HFM adds that FGI is offering a version of the codes in a free, searchable digital format.

## Takeaway

The 2026 FGI release is mostly a change in how requirements are organized and presented. Treat the codes as the requirement, the handbooks as the explanation, and confirm the enforced edition before updating project standards.
